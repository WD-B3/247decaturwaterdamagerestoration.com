Sudden Water Bill Jump in Decatur, AL: What It Can Mean

A bill that climbs with no change in habits may mean water is escaping somewhere. Here are the checks to run first.

Emergency Dispatch
(256) 667-9078
Call Now

By the 247 Decatur Water Damage Restoration team · Published November 10, 2026

A sudden water bill increase has several possible causes. A hidden leak is one. Others include seasonal use, irrigation, guests, a meter error or a rate change. The way to tell them apart is to check, not to guess.

This guide gives a few simple tests. They are not a diagnosis, and a plumber can confirm the source.

Compare your bills first

Look at the last six bills. Is the jump sudden or gradual? A sudden rise may point to a new leak or a running fixture. A gradual rise may be seasonal or tied to household changes.

Also check for extra usage you know about, such as filling a pool, watering a new lawn or a house guest staying for weeks.

A basic leak test

  • Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water.
  • Find the water meter and look at the leak indicator or low-flow dial.
  • If it moves with everything off, water is moving somewhere.
  • Note the reading, wait an hour with no water used and check again.
  • A changed reading suggests a leak.

If you find damp materials

A leak that has run for weeks may have soaked drywall, subfloor or insulation. Moisture can hide behind a wall. Moisture readings find it without cutting open the whole wall.

Shut off the water to the leaking fixture if you can. If you cannot find it, close the main valve and call a plumber. See slab leak water damage for one type of hidden leak.

Insurance and long leaks

Many policies treat slow, long-term leakage as gradual damage and may exclude it. A leak found early is easier to deal with than one found after months. Keep the bills, photos and plumber report.

Ask your insurer how your policy treats leaks that were not sudden.

Leaks outside the house

A leak does not have to be inside to raise a bill. A broken irrigation line, a cracked hose bib or a leaking line between the meter and the house can lose water without a stain indoors. Look for unusually green, soggy or sunken areas in the yard.

If the meter moves with everything off and you find nothing inside, the problem may be outdoors. A plumber can test the line between the meter and the house.

Toilets are the usual suspect

A toilet that keeps refilling can waste a lot of water without much noise. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks.

Replacing a flapper is a small, cheap repair. Do it early, before the bill becomes a surprise.

What to do with the bill

  • Call your water utility and ask for a usage history.
  • Ask whether the meter can be read or tested.
  • Ask about any leak adjustment policy and what proof they need.
  • Keep the plumber invoice and photos.
  • Check the next bill to confirm the repair worked.

Do not ignore a small change

A small, steady rise in the bill is easy to miss. A toilet or a dripping line can add up over months. Compare each bill with the same month last year, and ask the utility whether anything changed.

Early repair of a small leak is cheap. Late repair of a leak that soaked a wall is not.

When the bill is high but the meter is still

If the meter does not move with everything off, the usage may be real. Check for guests, a pool fill, new irrigation, a hose left running or a change in the household. If nothing explains it, ask the utility to review the meter and the reading.

What Decatur homeowners should know

Decatur Utilities provides water, wastewater, electricity and natural gas in Decatur. Its customer service can help read a meter and explain a bill. Ask whether it can compare your usage over time or check the meter.

Outside the city, billing may come from a different utility. The Town of Trinity has its own Water Department. If you live in Trinity, contact that office. In other nearby towns, check which utility serves your address.

When to call a professional

Call a plumber if the meter moves with everything off. Call a restoration company if you find damp walls, floors or ceilings. Both may be needed. The plumber stops the leak and we dry what it wet. If the leak is in the kitchen, see kitchen water damage. Homeowners in Tanner and elsewhere outside the city should also check which utility serves the address.

Do not wait for a stain. By then water has often traveled well beyond the source.

Decatur questions answered

Why did my water bill suddenly increase?

Causes include a hidden leak, a running fixture, irrigation, seasonal use or a meter issue. A meter test helps tell them apart.

How do I check for a leak with my meter?

Turn off all water use, read the meter, wait an hour and read it again. A change suggests a leak.

Who provides water in Decatur?

Decatur Utilities provides water, wastewater, electricity and natural gas in Decatur.

Will insurance cover a hidden leak?

Sudden leaks may be covered. Long-term leaks are often excluded. Read your policy.

What if I cannot find the leak?

Close the main valve and call a plumber. If materials are damp, call us to measure moisture.
