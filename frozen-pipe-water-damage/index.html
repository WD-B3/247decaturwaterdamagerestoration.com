Frozen pipe water damage in Decatur happens when ice blocks or splits a line and the leak starts as the pipe thaws. It is a smaller risk here than in colder states, but freezing nights do occur in the Tennessee Valley, and an unheated wall or crawl space is enough. Our line is answered 24/7, with same-day dispatch when crews are free.

This page covers the water after a freeze. Other supply failures are covered under burst pipe damage, and wet crawl spaces under crawl space water damage.

When a frozen pipe is an emergency

A pipe that is frozen but not yet leaking is a warning. A pipe that has thawed and is spraying is an emergency. Either way, the steps are the same.

Shut off the water

Close the main valve right away. A split pipe holds nothing back once the ice melts.

Do not use open flame

Never thaw a pipe with a torch or a propane heater. A hair dryer or warm towels are the safe way to warm a pipe that is still intact.

Look for the cold spot

Check exterior walls, crawl spaces, garages and under sinks on outside walls. That is where most freezes start.

Call a plumber and us

A plumber repairs the line. We remove the water and dry the structure.

How freezing damages a pipe

Water expands when it turns to ice. The pressure builds between the blockage and the closed faucet, and the pipe splits there rather than where the ice sits. That is why the leak often appears away from the frozen spot and only after the thaw.

Copper, PEX and CPVC all fail in different ways. Copper splits along a seam, and rigid plastic can crack. We note the pipe type and the break location in our written scope for your plumber and your adjuster.

The honest picture for Decatur

Decatur sees freezing nights most winters, but hard freezes are less common than in the north, and many homes were not built with that risk in mind. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages and vented crawl spaces are the weak points. The National Weather Service office in Huntsville publishes local climate data if you want the numbers for your area.

We do not guess how often it happens at your address. We look at where the water came from and what the readings show.

How we dry and repair after a freeze

We extract water first and then locate wet materials with moisture meters and thermal imaging. Splits inside walls are often hidden until a stain shows, so we scan wide.

Affected drywall, insulation and flooring are removed or dried as the readings require. If the home is vacant over a winter weekend, ask us about documenting before drying begins, because the claim often depends on timing. Rebuild work follows the same path as wall and drywall repair and structural drying.

Insurance and a frozen pipe

Freeze damage is often treated as sudden and accidental, but policies may ask whether you kept the heat on or drained the lines in a vacant home. Read the wording or ask your insurer before you decide how to proceed.

Photograph the frozen pipe and the split, note the date and temperature, and keep any plumber invoice. Do not throw away the damaged section until your adjuster agrees.

What affects the cost

Time before shut-off

Water that ran for an hour is a small job. Water that ran for a day while the house was empty is a large one.

Which materials got wet

Ceilings and insulation are heavy and slow to dry. A single split under a sink is smaller.

Hidden areas

Wall cavities need to be opened and checked before drying equipment is placed.

Drying days

Cold, damp weather slows drying, which can add days of equipment.

Rebuild after

Replacement drywall, paint and flooring are separate from the cleanup.

Freeze risk in older Decatur homes

Old Decatur holds the largest Victorian-era home district in Alabama, and homes of that age were built before modern insulation practice, so any supply line that sits in an exterior wall or an unheated space is the first to feel a hard freeze.

If you own one, insulating exposed lines and sealing gaps around pipe penetrations is cheaper than a ceiling repair. Ask a licensed plumber about heat tape or foam insulation, and check with the City of Decatur Building Department whether any pipe work needs a permit.

Frozen Pipe Water Damage questions answered

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Decatur?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7 and crews go out the same day if crews can reach you. Freeze leaks often show up at night as the temperature rises, so after-hours calls are normal for us.

My pipe is frozen but not leaking. Should I call you?

Not yet. Close the water at the main, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or towels and call a plumber if it will not thaw. Call us if water appears or you see a stain.

Why did my pipe leak after it thawed?

The ice itself often seals a split. When it melts, the water has somewhere to go. That is why damage often appears hours after the cold snap ends.

Does Decatur really get cold enough for frozen pipes?

Freezing nights occur most winters, and unheated spaces get colder than the weather report. Hard freezes are less frequent, so it is a lower-frequency risk than in northern states. When it does happen the damage is real.

Do I need to remove all the wet drywall?

Not always. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water actually went. Drywall that stayed wet or sagged usually comes out, and drywall with a small stain can sometimes be dried in place.
