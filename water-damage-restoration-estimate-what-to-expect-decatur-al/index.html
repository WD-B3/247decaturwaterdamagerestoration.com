What a Water Damage Estimate Includes in Decatur, AL

A good estimate lists the work, the rooms and the equipment. Here is what to look for before you agree to anything.

By the 247 Decatur Water Damage Restoration team · Published November 10, 2026

A water damage restoration estimate should tell you what will be done, where and why. It should not be a single number with no detail. We do not quote prices on this page because every loss is different, but we can explain what a clear estimate contains so you can compare offers.

Ask for the estimate in writing. If a company will not provide one before work begins, that tells you something.

The parts of a clear estimate

SectionWhat it should say
ScopeRooms, materials and areas affected
Water categoryClean, gray or black water and the reason
ExtractionMethod and areas covered
RemovalMaterials to be taken out and why
DryingEquipment type, count and expected days
MonitoringHow often readings are taken
ExclusionsWhat is not included
RebuildWhether it is included or separate

Why equipment counts and days matter

Drying equipment is usually billed per unit per day. An estimate should say how many air movers and dehumidifiers are planned, and for how many days. Drying is measured, so the actual number may change, and the company should explain how it will adjust.

Industry guidance suggests placing air movers based on the affected area and wall length. A plan that does not mention placement or readings is a sign to ask more questions. See structural drying for what the equipment does.

Extraction and removal

Extraction removes standing water and as much as possible from carpet, pad and floors. Removal takes out materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or ceiling drywall.

The estimate should say what is being removed and what is staying. Removal is where costs change most, because hidden moisture can extend the area once the wall is open. Ask how supplemental charges are approved. See water extraction for the first stage.

Contents, cleaning and disposal

Some estimates leave out contents handling, cleaning and disposal. Others include them. Ask whether moving furniture, packing out belongings or hauling debris is in the price.

A rug, a rolling cart of books or a storage box on a wet floor may add time. Moving things before the crew arrives can reduce the work. Do not move heavy furniture alone and do not lift anything from contaminated water.

What an estimate cannot tell you up front

A first estimate is based on what can be seen and measured. Hidden moisture, an unknown source or a slow leak can change the scope. Good companies tell you which numbers are firm and which depend on what is found.

If something changes, you should hear about it before the work is done, not after. Ask how change orders are written and who signs them.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Is this estimate a fixed price, a range or time and materials?
  • What equipment is planned, and how many days?
  • How will drying be verified?
  • What is excluded?
  • Who handles permits, if needed?
  • Is rebuild included, and who does it?
  • How are changes approved?

Insurance and the estimate

If you are filing a claim, your insurer may write its own estimate. The two may differ. Ask the company for line item detail, photos and moisture logs, since those are what an adjuster reviews.

We do not guarantee that an insurer will pay any amount. We provide records, such as the photos and readings gathered during structural drying.

How to compare two estimates

Put the two scopes side by side and compare rooms, equipment counts and drying days. A lower total may simply mean fewer rooms, fewer machines or no monitoring.

Ask each company to explain any line the other one lacks. The answers usually show which estimate is complete and which one will grow later.

What Decatur homeowners should know

Costs and scope depend on the home. Older houses in the Old Decatur district may include plaster, wood framing and older plumbing, while newer homes in Trinity or Mooresville may have different finishes and slab or crawl space construction.

The City of Decatur Building Department lists repairs and remodeling of existing structures among work that needs a permit. Ask your contractor whether your repair requires one and who will pull it.

Decatur questions answered

What should a water damage estimate include?

A clear scope by room, the water category, the equipment and drying days, monitoring, exclusions and whether the rebuild is included.

Why do water damage estimates change?

Hidden moisture can extend the affected area once walls or floors are opened. A good company explains when and how changes are approved.

Is the estimate the same as what insurance pays?

No. An insurer writes its own estimate and may differ. Records such as photos and moisture logs help.

Should the estimate list drying equipment?

Yes. It should state the type and number of units and the expected days, and explain how readings will adjust the plan.

Can I get the estimate in writing before work starts?

You should. If a company refuses, treat that as a warning sign.
