Water damage restoration in Tanner, AL covers a community about nine miles north of Decatur and four miles south of Athens. Tanner is an unincorporated community in Limestone County, and storm season is the main reason people call. The emergency line answers at all hours, and a crew heads out from Decatur on the same day if schedules allow.

Because there is no town hall, the practical notes below fill that gap. Start with what to do when water is spreading.

When water is spreading in Tanner

If water is coming into your home after a storm or a pipe failure, take these steps first.

Stop the source

Close the shut-off valve on a failed line, or the main valve at the house.

Stay clear of power

After a storm, treat downed lines as live. Switch off breakers only when the panel is reachable on dry ground.

Cover openings if safe

A tarp or board over a broken window or roof opening limits rain. Do not climb wet roofs.

Photograph first

Capture damage before cleanup for your claim.

Storm season first

Tanner has been in the path of severe weather before. The April 3, 1974 Super Outbreak included violent tornadoes that struck the Tanner area, and the community lies in the same storm-prone Tennessee Valley region as Decatur. Spring is the season to prepare.

Wind and hail open roofs, and rain follows the opening. Our storm water damage cleanup page explains what we dry and what a roofer handles, and ceiling repair covers the interior damage that follows a roof leak.

Emergency dispatch

A railroad community, now a commuter one

Tanner was settled along the Louisville and Nashville Railroad and was first called McDonald's Station, then Rowland. The Tanner post office opened in 1913 and was named for Samuel Tanner, Athens' first mayor. Elevation is about 666 feet and the climate is humid subtropical.

Whether a home sits over a crawl space or on a concrete slab decides which water problems apply. See crawl space water damage and slab leaks for each.

How crews reach Tanner

Crews drive north on US 31 across the Tennessee River from Decatur. After heavy rain or a storm, roads and the river crossing can slow traffic. We never promise an arrival time. We tell you the plan and update you if conditions change.

We also serve Trinity to the southwest and Mooresville to the southeast. The Decatur home page describes the whole service.

Calling from Tanner

Tell the dispatcher your road and the nearest landmark, since rural addresses can be hard to find at night. If water is still running, say so first, because that decides what equipment comes.

A crew drives out from Decatur, so travel time depends on traffic and call volume. We offer same-day response when available and we do not promise a fixed arrival time.

What to do while you wait

  • Close the shutoff valve at the leaking fixture, or the main valve at the meter.
  • Lift small items and rugs off wet flooring.
  • Photograph each room before you move anything.
  • Keep children and pets out of standing water.
  • Leave wet drywall alone until the crew takes readings.

No town hall: who handles what in Tanner

Tanner is unincorporated, so there is no town hall or town building department. For permit questions on repair work, ask Limestone County. For water service, ask your water utility, since providers vary by address.

Do not assume the City of Decatur Building Department handles Tanner. Photograph, keep receipts and ask us for a written scope.

Insurance and storms

Wind and hail damage is often covered by homeowners policies, sometimes with a separate deductible. Flood is normally excluded and has to be bought as separate flood coverage. Ask your insurer how your policy treats it.
