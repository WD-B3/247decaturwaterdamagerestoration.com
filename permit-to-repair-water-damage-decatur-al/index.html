Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Decatur, AL?

Drying usually does not need one. Rebuilding sometimes does. Here is how to find out.

(256) 667-9078
Call Now

By the 247 Decatur Water Damage Restoration team · Published November 10, 2026

Do you need a permit to repair water damage? Sometimes. Emergency water removal and drying generally do not involve construction, while rebuilding walls, replacing electrical or plumbing, or changing structure often does. We are not the permitting authority, so this guide explains the pattern and tells you who to ask.

The rules depend on the jurisdiction and the work. The only reliable answer comes from the office that issues permits where your home is.

What usually does not need a permit

  • Extracting water and running drying equipment.
  • Removing wet carpet, pad and loose materials.
  • Cleaning and sanitizing surfaces.
  • Cosmetic work such as painting.

What often does

WorkWhy it may need a permit
Replacing electrical wiring or panelsSafety inspection
Replacing plumbing linesCode compliance
Structural repair of framingLoad and safety
Reroofing or major roof repairWeather protection and code
Rebuilding walls and finishes after large lossesRepair or remodel of an existing structure
Water heater replacementGas, venting and pressure safety

Who pulls the permit

In many places the contractor pulls the permit for the trade they perform, and a homeowner may pull one for work on their own home. Ask who is responsible, and ask for a copy for your records.

Unpermitted work can create trouble at resale or in an insurance claim. It is easier to ask first than to fix it later.

Inspections and timing

If a permit is required, an inspection may be needed before walls are closed. That means timing matters. If the inspector must see the framing or wiring, drywall waits.

Plan the order of work with your contractor. Drying first, inspection second, rebuild third is a common sequence. See structural drying for the first stage.

Licensing is a separate question

A permit and a license are different. A permit covers the work. A license covers the contractor. Alabama sets licensing rules by type of work and cost, and you can check a contractor with the licensing board.

Ask for the license number and look it up. Do not rely on a business card.

Permits, insurance and resale

An insurer may ask for permits on larger repairs, and a buyer may ask for them when you sell. Missing paperwork can slow a claim or a closing.

Keep the permit, the inspection sign-off and the final invoice together. They show the work was done to code and by whom.

Floodplain rules matter after a flood

Homes in a floodplain face extra rules. Floodplain management regulations can apply when damage is substantial, and they can affect how and whether you rebuild. The City of Decatur Building Department enforces floodplain management for property inside the city.

Call before you start rebuilding. Ask whether the damage level triggers special requirements, and ask what documents the office wants. Our flood damage restoration page explains the physical cleanup.

Working with a contractor on permits

Many contractors pull permits for their own work. Ask whether the permit fee is in the price, who schedules inspections and who is on site for them. A contractor who avoids the subject is a warning sign.

Ask for the permit number once it is issued and check it with the office. Do not start rebuild work that needs a permit before it is approved.

If work was already done without a permit

If a prior owner or contractor skipped a required permit, ask the Building Department what it would take to correct it. Some offices allow after-the-fact permits, and some require inspection of finished work.

Do not guess. A short call now can prevent a problem at closing, or at a later claim.

A simple order of operations

  • Stop the water and document the damage.
  • Extract and dry the structure.
  • Ask the permitting office what the rebuild needs.
  • Have the permit issued before rebuild starts.
  • Schedule inspections before closing walls.
  • File the final paperwork with your records.

Keep your own paper trail

Write down the date you called the office, who answered and what they said. If rules or staff change, your notes show you acted in good faith. Photos of finished work before drywall closes are worth keeping too.

What Decatur homeowners should know

The City of Decatur Building Department issues permits for work inside the city and treats repair and remodel work on existing structures as permit work. It also enforces floodplain management regulations, which matters if your home is in a flood-prone area. Call the department with your address and describe the work.

Properties outside city limits may fall under a different authority. In Mooresville, Trinity and other nearby towns, the answer can differ, so ask the local office that covers your address.

Before you call the office

  • Have your address and parcel information ready.
  • Describe the work in plain terms.
  • Ask whether the property is in a floodplain.
  • Ask what inspections are required.
  • Ask who may pull the permit.
  • Write down the answer and the name of the person you spoke to.

Decatur questions answered

Do I need a permit for water extraction?

Extraction and drying are generally not construction. Rebuilding may need a permit, so ask the Building Department.

Who do I call in Decatur about a permit?

The City of Decatur Building Department issues permits for work inside the city.

Does a plumber need a permit to replace a pipe?

Often yes for replacement work. Ask your plumber and the permitting office.

What if my home is outside Decatur city limits?

A different authority may apply. Ask the office that covers your address.

Is a permit the same as a license?

No. A permit covers the work and a license covers the contractor. Check both.
