How a water damage job runs

Every job follows the same order. The steps repeat on large losses and small ones, and only the equipment changes.

1. Call and describe it

Tell the dispatcher what happened, where the water came from and whether it is still running. If you can reach the shutoff safely, close it.

2. Assess and map moisture

The crew checks the affected rooms with moisture meters and decides what water category applies. It explains the plan before moving equipment in.

3. Extract and remove

Standing water comes out first. Materials that cannot be dried, such as saturated pad or ceiling drywall, are taken out and bagged.

4. Dry with equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the target. The crew checks them on set visits and logs the numbers.

5. Verify and document

When the structure is dry, equipment leaves. You receive photos and readings that your insurer can review.

What affects the cost of water damage

We do not publish flat prices, because water damage is not flat. Four things move the price most.

How much is wet

A single room costs less to dry than a floor. Square footage and wall height set the equipment count.

What kind of water

Clean supply water costs less to handle than gray water or sewage, which need removal and sanitizing.

How long it sat

Fresh water stays in the surface layers. Water that sat for days has moved into framing and subfloor.

What must be removed

Drying in place costs less than tear-out. Readings decide which one a material needs.

Insurance and water damage

Many water damage losses are paid under a homeowners policy when the cause is sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe or a failed hose. Flooding is different. The Alabama Department of Insurance says flooding is not typically covered through most homeowners policies, and flood coverage is bought separately.

We do not file claims and we cannot promise that a claim will be paid. We give you photos, moisture readings and a written scope. Our guide on homeowners insurance and water damage explains the general pattern.

Local conditions we plan around

Decatur sits on the Tennessee River and Wheeler Lake, and Flint Creek runs through the area. The ground is a mix of hills and flat low areas, so water behaves differently from street to street. Humid air in the valley also slows drying, so dehumidifiers do much of the work.

The National Weather Service tracks the Tennessee River at a gauge in Decatur, and flood stages begin at 9 feet. The City of Decatur Building Department issues permits for repairs of existing structures and enforces floodplain management, so ask it whether your rebuild needs one. Older homes in the Old Decatur historic district bring plaster, wood framing and older plumbing, and we plan the drying around them.

Questions Decatur homeowners ask

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Decatur?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. We offer same-day response when available and explain the scope and price before work starts.

How fast should water damage be dried?

As soon as possible. Water spreads into drywall, flooring and framing within hours, and drying works best when it starts early.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Sudden and accidental losses such as a burst pipe are often covered, while flooding is typically not. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

What areas do you serve besides Decatur?

We serve Trinity, Mooresville, Tanner and Somerville, and the roads between them.

What should I do before the crew arrives?

Shut off the water if it is safe, turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry, photograph the damage and move small valuables out of the water.

How do I get a price for water damage work?

Call the line and describe what happened. We explain the scope and price before work starts, and we do not quote a flat number before we know what is wet.
