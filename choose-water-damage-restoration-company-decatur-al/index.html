How to Choose a Water Damage Company in Decatur, AL

You may have to choose fast. These questions help you pick a company that works to a standard and puts it in writing.

Emergency Dispatch
(256) 667-9078
Call Now

By the 247 Decatur Water Damage Restoration team · Published November 10, 2026

Choosing a water damage restoration company is hard when water is on the floor. You may have little time. A few questions can separate a careful company from one that only wants the job. We will tell you what we would ask if we were the customer.

No company should pressure you. You decide, and you should have a written scope before work starts.

Questions to ask

  • Do you work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard?
  • Will you measure moisture and keep a log?
  • Can you give me a written scope before you start?
  • What is not included?
  • Who will be at my home, and are they your employees?
  • Are you licensed and insured, and can I see proof?
  • How do you handle permits and rebuild?

Check credentials yourself

Ask for license and insurance information and verify it. The Alabama Department of Insurance advises getting multiple bids, asking for references and verifying licenses, permits, insurance and bonding. The Home Builders Licensure Board offers a license lookup.

A certificate emailed from a company is not as good as a lookup you do yourself. Take five minutes.

Be careful of these signals

Door-to-door offers after a storm, requests for large up-front payments, pressure to sign over insurance benefits and promises that the claim will be paid are common warning signs. A vague scope is another.

Take your time where you can. Stopping the water and calling your insurer are both good first steps while you compare.

Speed and quality are not opposites

Water damage is time-sensitive, so a quick response matters. But a quick response is not the same as a rushed one. A crew should still take readings, explain the plan and answer questions.

We aim to respond the same day as soon as a crew frees up, and we explain the scope and price before work starts. We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic, weather and call volume vary.

Reading reviews and references with care

Online ratings are a start, but they can be thin or hard to verify. Ask the company for two recent customers you can call, and ask what the job looked like when it finished.

Look for details in what past customers say. Comments that mention readings, clear scopes and fair communication are more useful than a bare star count. Never rely on a company that has no phone number or local address.

Insurance and the company you pick

Your insurer may suggest a vendor, but you generally choose your contractor. Tell your adjuster who you hired, send the scope and keep the drying log. If the adjuster disputes a line, a clear record helps settle it.

Avoid signing over your claim rights or benefits to anyone until you understand the document. Read before you sign and keep a copy.

A short checklist to keep by the phone

  • Company name, address and local phone number.
  • License and insurance details, verified by you.
  • Written scope and exclusions.
  • Equipment count and planned drying days.
  • Plan for readings and logs.
  • Who handles rebuild and permits.

What to do if you already signed

If you signed under pressure, read the contract again. Look for cancellation terms and ask the company to explain anything unclear. Contact your insurer before more work is done, and keep every document.

You can still ask for a written scope, readings and a drying log. A fair company provides them. If it refuses, speak to your insurer and consider getting a second opinion on what has been done.

Trust what you can verify

Anyone can say they are licensed and insured. Ask for the license number, look it up and call the insurer if you want proof of coverage. It takes minutes and filters out most bad actors.

A good company expects the questions and answers them calmly.

What Decatur homeowners should know

After a Decatur storm or flood, out-of-area crews sometimes travel in and knock on doors. Ask where the company is based, whether it has a local office and how to reach it after the job. A local business is easier to hold accountable.

We are based in Decatur and also serve Trinity, Mooresville, Tanner and Somerville. For emergencies see water extraction.

What to expect when the crew arrives

A good crew introduces itself, walks the home with you, takes moisture readings and explains what it found. It should tell you the plan before moving equipment in.

Expect extraction first, then removal where needed and then drying equipment, as described on structural drying. You should receive documentation as the job moves along.

Decatur questions answered

How do I choose a water damage restoration company?

Ask about standards, moisture logs, a written scope, exclusions, licensing and insurance, and verify the credentials yourself.

Should I get more than one bid?

Yes when you can. The Alabama Department of Insurance advises getting multiple bids and checking references.

Is a fast response a good sign?

Yes if the crew still takes readings and explains the plan. Speed without records is not enough.

Should I pay up front?

Be cautious about large up-front payments. Ask for a written scope and payment terms.

Do I have to use my insurer's recommended company?

Generally you choose your contractor. Ask your insurer how your policy handles it.
