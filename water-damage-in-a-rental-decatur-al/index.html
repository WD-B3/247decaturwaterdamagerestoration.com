Water Damage in a Decatur, AL Rental: Tenants and Landlords

When a rental floods or leaks, speed and records matter for both sides. Here is a practical guide.

Emergency Dispatch
(256) 667-9078
Call Now

By the 247 Decatur Water Damage Restoration team · Published November 10, 2026

Water damage in a rental raises questions for both sides. The tenant wants safety and a dry home. The landlord wants to protect the building. Neither benefits from delay. We are not attorneys, and the lease and state law decide responsibilities, so this guide covers practical steps.

The common thread is documentation. Whoever finds the problem should write it down and tell the other party quickly.

What a tenant should do first

  • Shut off water if you can do so safely, using the fixture valve or the main.
  • Take photos and video of the damage and the source.
  • Notify the landlord or property manager in writing, with the date and time.
  • Move belongings out of the wet area.
  • Keep receipts for emergency costs.
  • Do not throw away damaged items until photographed.

What a landlord should do

Respond quickly and keep a record of when you were told and what you did. Get a plumber to stop the source and a restoration company to dry the structure. Delay lets damage grow and can affect the next tenant and your insurance.

Notify your property insurer. A tenant's renters policy may not cover the building, and the landlord's policy may not cover the tenant's belongings.

Mold and health worries

A damp rental can grow mold if it stays wet. Dry quickly and ask for moisture readings. Tenants worried about health can speak with a doctor and the landlord. We do not offer mold services, and we focus on water removal and drying.

If water is from sewage or flood, avoid contact and wait for professional cleanup. See sewage backup cleanup.

Keeping the relationship workable

Both sides do better when they communicate in writing and set dates. A short email that lists what happened, what has been done and what comes next prevents misunderstandings.

If there is a dispute, keep your records and consider legal advice or a tenant or landlord resource in your area.

If the unit is not livable

Large losses can make a rental unusable for days or weeks. Whether rent is reduced, or the tenant is moved, depends on the lease and the law. Talk to the landlord in writing and keep every message.

Tenants with renters insurance should check for coverage of added living costs. Landlords should check their own policy for rental income loss. Neither should assume.

Preventing the next leak

  • Replace old washing machine and dishwasher hoses.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets every few months.
  • Test the AC drain line before summer.
  • Report slow drips early rather than waiting.
  • Keep a simple log of repairs and inspections.

Who calls us

We take calls from both tenants and landlords. If a tenant calls, we ask for the landlord or manager's approval before beginning work other than emergency steps. Landlords can call and authorize the whole scope.

For multi-unit buildings, water often reaches neighboring units. Tell us which units are affected so the crew can check them.

Respect each other's roles

Tenants should report promptly and allow access for repair. Landlords should respond promptly and give notice before entering. Clear, polite, written messages keep things calm.

Both sides gain when the structure dries quickly and the record is complete.

Mold worries in a rental

A damp rental can raise mold concerns. Tenants should report moisture quickly. Landlords should dry the area promptly. We handle the water side and do not provide mold services, so ask a qualified professional about any growth you see.

Photos at move-in and move-out

Tenants who photograph the unit at move-in and landlords who inspect between tenants both create records that help sort out future water damage questions fairly.

What Decatur renters and landlords should know

Decatur has many apartments, duplexes and older houses used as rentals. Older buildings may have aging supply lines and water heaters. See water heater leak cleanup and appliance leak damage for common sources.

We serve rentals as well as owner-occupied homes. If you are in Mooresville or Somerville, call the same line. The landlord usually authorizes the work, so tenants should tell the landlord before a crew arrives.

Steps for a clean paper trail

  • Write the date and time you discovered the problem.
  • Send written notice by email or text and keep a copy.
  • Ask for a response date.
  • Photograph the repair at each stage.
  • Keep invoices and drying logs.
  • Confirm in writing when the repair is complete.

Decatur questions answered

Who is responsible for water damage in a rental?

The lease and state law decide. Generally the landlord handles the building and the tenant handles personal belongings.

Should a tenant call a restoration company?

Tell the landlord first. The landlord usually authorizes the work, though a tenant can call for emergency help.

Does renters insurance cover water damage?

It may cover belongings if the cause is covered, but usually not the building. Read the policy.

What should a tenant document?

Photos, video, dates, written notices to the landlord and receipts for emergency costs.

Can a landlord be late in responding?

Delay can make damage worse. Both sides should act quickly and keep written records.
