Shower Pan Leak Water Damage: A Decatur, AL Guide

A leaking shower pan sends water into the subfloor and the room below. Here is how to spot it and what happens next.

Emergency Dispatch
(256) 667-9078
Call Now

By the Decatur Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Shower pan leak water damage starts under the tile. The pan is the waterproof layer beneath the shower floor, and when it fails water seeps into the subfloor, framing and the ceiling below. Because the pan is hidden, many leaks run for months before anyone sees a stain.

This guide explains the signs, how a leak is confirmed and what the repair and drying involve. It is general information, not a diagnosis of your shower.

Signs a shower pan is leaking

The clearest sign is a stain on the ceiling below the bathroom. Other signs are quieter.

  • A ceiling stain or bubble below a second-floor shower.
  • Grout that stays dark, cracks or crumbles near the base.
  • Tile that loosens or sounds hollow when tapped.
  • A musty smell in the bathroom or the room below.
  • Baseboards or wall bottoms that stain next to the shower.
  • A water bill that creeps up with no other cause.

How a leak is confirmed

A plumber or tile contractor usually runs a flood test. The drain is plugged and the pan is filled with water, and the level is marked. If it drops, the pan or the drain seal is leaking. Moisture readings in the surrounding walls and floor show where the water went.

We can measure moisture and map where it traveled. We do not rebuild the shower, and we do not replace the pan. A tile or plumbing contractor does that, and we dry what the water reached.

Drying and the room below

Water that leaks from a shower pan usually ends up in the subfloor and sometimes the ceiling below. We extract, remove wet insulation and drywall as readings require and dry the cavity with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. The ceiling repair is covered on our ceiling water damage repair page.

Do not close the wall or the ceiling over damp materials. Drying first prevents new problems.

Insurance and a shower pan leak

A shower pan that has leaked slowly for a long time is often treated as gradual damage, which many policies exclude. A sudden failure, such as a supply line, may be covered. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Keep the plumber report, photos and moisture readings. They show cause and timing.

Why shower leaks stay hidden

A shower pan sits under the tile and mortar bed, so the leak has to pass through several layers before it shows. Water can soak the subfloor, run along joists and appear far from the shower. A stain on a ceiling may be several feet away from the leak.

The delay is the problem. Wet wood and drywall stay damp for weeks if no one dries them, and a long, slow leak is the type many policies treat as gradual.

Immediate steps if you suspect a leak

  • Stop using the shower until a contractor checks it.
  • Photograph any ceiling stain and note when you first saw it.
  • Place a bucket under active drips and keep pets away from wet areas.
  • Do not cut into the ceiling unless water is pooling and a contractor advises it.
  • Call a plumber or tile contractor for a flood test.
  • Call us if drywall, insulation or subfloor may be wet.

What we do and what we leave to others

We find and map moisture, extract standing water, remove wet material that cannot be dried and run dehumidifiers and air movers. We document readings every visit.

We do not set tile or replace pans. A tile contractor rebuilds the shower after the cavity is dry. Coordinating the sequence keeps the new tile from sealing in moisture. See structural drying for the process.

Preventing a repeat

Ask the contractor about waterproofing the whole shower, not just the pan, and about the drain seal. A properly sloped base and a flood test before tile goes on are cheap checks that catch problems early.

Watch your caulk and grout and fix gaps quickly. Small gaps let water in over time. Look at the ceiling below a shower once in a while, since stains show up there first.

What Decatur homeowners should know

The City of Decatur Building Department counts repairs and remodels of existing structures as permit work. Ask whether a shower rebuild does. The Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board sets a $10,000 threshold for residential home builder licensing, which applies when total cost exceeds that amount.

Old Decatur has the largest Victorian-era home district in Alabama, and older homes may have plumbing and framing that complicate a bathroom repair. If your home is in the area, ask the contractor about it. We also serve Tanner and Trinity.

Decatur questions answered

How do I know if my shower pan is leaking?

Look for a stain on the ceiling below, loose tile, dark grout and a musty smell. A flood test by a contractor confirms it.

Can I fix a shower pan leak myself?

Surface regrouting is possible, but a failed pan needs a rebuild. A contractor can test and repair it.

Does insurance cover a leaking shower pan?

Slow leaks are often excluded, while sudden failures may be covered. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

How long does the damage take to dry?

Several days is common. Readings decide, not the calendar.

Do I need a permit to replace a shower pan in Decatur?

The Building Department lists repairs and remodeling among work that needs a permit. Ask the department about your specific project.
