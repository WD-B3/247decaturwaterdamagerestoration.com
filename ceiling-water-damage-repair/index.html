Ceiling water damage repair in Decatur covers the finished ceiling and everything above it, including insulation and framing. A stain that spreads, a bubble in the paint or a slow drip all mean water is sitting in the cavity. Our line is answered 24/7, and we send a crew the same day if crews can reach you.

The cause can be a roof, a pipe, a bathroom above or a condensate line. Many roof leaks start as a tiny failure. If the water came with a storm, our page on storm water damage cleanup adds more detail.

A sagging ceiling is an emergency

A ceiling that bows or holds water can fail suddenly, and wet drywall is heavy. Stay out from under it, and move people, pets and furniture away. If you can do it safely, puncturing a small hole at the lowest point with a screwdriver and catching the water in a bucket can relieve weight, but only if no electrical fixture is nearby.

Stay out from under it

Keep people clear of any ceiling that sags or bubbles. Wet drywall can drop without warning.

Turn off nearby power

Cut the breaker for lights or fans in the area if the panel is dry to reach. Water and wiring do not mix.

Catch and contain

Place buckets, move furniture and cover valuables with plastic. Do not run a light fixture that is wet.

Find the source if safe

Note whether it rained, whether a bathroom or air handler sits above and whether the stain grows.

Finding where the water came from

The stain shows where water ended up, not where it started. We trace it with moisture meters and thermal imaging, and open the ceiling where needed to see insulation and framing. A roof leak usually leaves staining that follows a rafter line. A plumbing leak often leaves a ring that grows after use of a fixture, and an air handler leak tends to stain near a return or vent.

In older plaster ceilings, such as those in the Old Decatur Victorian district, water can travel along lath and appear far from the leak. Plaster over wood lath was common in homes of that era, and it behaves differently from modern drywall when wet.

Roof leaks and the ceilings under them

A roof leak usually shows up first as a ceiling problem, so it is handled here. Most start at flashing around a chimney, vent or skylight, at a failed shingle or at a valley. Water runs under the roofing, wets the decking and insulation, and shows up in the ceiling below.

We do not replace roofs. A roofing contractor handles that. We dry the cavity, remove wet insulation and drywall and repair the ceiling after the roof is fixed. Keep photos of the stain, the roof and the date the leak started. They matter for a claim. Our wall and drywall repair page explains how related damage gets handled.

Repair options, and the trade-offs

A small, dry stain may only need sealing and paint. Wet or sagging drywall is cut out, and insulation is replaced. A larger area is often easier and cleaner to remove than to patch.

Dry stain, no sagSeal, prime and paintLowest cost, but only if the leak is fixed and dry

Insurance and ceiling leaks

Sudden roof and plumbing leaks are often covered, while long-term seepage and neglected maintenance often are not. The roof itself may follow a different rule from the interior damage, so read your policy or ask your insurer.

Document the stain before cleanup starts. We write a scope that lists the removed ceiling area and the drying readings.

Ceiling Water Damage Repair questions answered

Do you handle ceiling leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Decatur?

Yes. Our line is answered 24/7, including weekends and holidays. Call, say whether the ceiling is sagging and whether water is still coming through.

Can I just paint over a ceiling stain?

Only after the leak is fixed and the ceiling is dry. Paint over a wet stain traps moisture and usually shows through again. We check moisture first.

Should I pop a sagging ceiling to drain the water?

It can reduce weight, but it is risky. Use a small hole at the lowest point, stay clear of fixtures and wiring and keep a bucket ready. If you are unsure, stay out and call.

Do you repair the roof?

No. A roofing contractor repairs the roof. We handle drying, removal and ceiling repair, and we coordinate timing so the leak is stopped first.

How do I know whether it is the roof or a pipe?

A stain that appears or grows after rain points to the roof. A stain that grows after a shower or washing machine cycle points to plumbing. We use moisture readings to confirm.
