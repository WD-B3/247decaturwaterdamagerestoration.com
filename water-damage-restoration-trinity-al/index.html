When water is spreading in Trinity

If water is moving through your house right now, work through these before the crew arrives. They take minutes and they cut the damage.

Stop the source

Shut the valve at the leaking fixture, or close the main where the line enters the house. If the leak is on the street side of the meter, report it to the town.

Power off only if dry

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Otherwise stay out and call the electric utility.

Lift what you can

Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water. Foil under furniture legs limits stains.

Keep people out of dirty water

If water came from a drain or sewer, keep children and pets away and call us.

Living on a ridge: what water does in Trinity

The southern parts of Trinity sit on Trinity Mountain, a broad ridge that rises several hundred feet above the surrounding terrain. Town elevation is about 679 feet, higher than Decatur at 594. Plantation owners built on the mountain in the 1810s to get away from the mosquito-infested lowlands.

What that means today is practical. A house on a ridge tends to shed runoff rather than collect it, so roof leaks, clogged gutters and wind-driven rain are often a bigger concern than rising water. The low ground toward Wheeler Lake north of US 72 is a different situation, and flood damage cleanup covers it.

Water service and jurisdiction in Trinity

The Town of Trinity runs its own water department, with the town hall at 35 Preston Drive. If a leak is on the utility side of your meter, report it to the town. Trinity is a separate town from Decatur, so permit questions for repair work go to the town, not to the City of Decatur Building Department.

Dispatch from Decatur also reaches Hillsboro to the west and Belle Mina on the Limestone County side. Neither is a separate page on this site, so call the same line.

Insurance in plain terms

A burst line or a failed hose is the kind of sudden event homeowners policies often pay for, and slow seepage or neglected plumbing is the kind they often do not. Flood damage typically falls outside a homeowners policy and needs its own flood coverage. Read your policy or ask your insurer before you decide.

Photograph the damage before cleanup, and keep receipts. We provide a written scope for your insurer.
