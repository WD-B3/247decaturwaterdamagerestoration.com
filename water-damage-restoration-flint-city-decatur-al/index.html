First steps for a wet home

Stop inside water

Close the valve at the fixture or the main if the water is coming from a pipe.

Watch for rising water

If water is coming in from outside, do not wade through it. Get yourself and your family to safety and call 911 if you are in danger.

Protect your footing

Stay away from outlets and cords in standing water. Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry.

Document

Photograph the water line and the damage before cleanup.

What we can and cannot say about drainage

A city drainage and flooding report exists for Flint City Park. That report concerns the park itself. We do not treat it as evidence about nearby homes, and we do not claim that your street floods.

What we can do is look at your house. Crews check where water entered, how far it spread and whether the crawl space, subfloor or walls are still wet. Heavy outdoor flooding is covered on our flood damage page.

Moisture that stays after the water is gone

After a wet spell, damp air under a house can keep floors wet. Look for cupped boards, a musty smell or condensation on ducts. Our page on crawl space water damage explains what we measure and when drying works.

A below-grade room is different. Wet carpet and walls in a lower level are on the basement page. Storm entry points are on the storm damage page.

Insurance, flood zones and mold

Many homeowners policies exclude flood and cover sudden pipe failures, and the cause decides which applies. Policies differ, so ask your insurer. We cannot promise coverage. We provide photos and moisture records.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Wall cavities and crawl spaces that stay damp can grow mold within a day or two, which is why drying speed matters. We neither test for nor remove mold.
